## THE CORE QUESTION: WERE ANY FAMILY MEMBERS BUFFALO HUNTERS?

**Short answer, with evidence status:**

- **DOCUMENTED (yes, in-law line):** **William Peter Hallett (1811–1873)** — Catherine (Hallett) Spence's **paternal half-uncle** — was a **documented, elected buffalo-hunt captain** and the elected leader of the English-speaking Half-Breed buffalo hunt out of Red River in the 1860s, later Chief Scout of the Boundary Commission's "49th Rangers" (1872–73). He was an in-law to the Spence line, not a Spence by blood. **[VERIFIED — Barkwell bio]**
- **NOT DOCUMENTED for the blood lines:** No source was found naming **any Spence, Setter, or Riggs** as a buffalo hunter. The family's documented livelihoods are HBC service (tailor, labourer/voyageur), farming, fur trading, politics (MLA/JP). **[VERIFIED as absent — see Part 2]**
- **HIGHLY LIKELY but INFERENCE:** David Spence (1824–1885, Poplar Point/Portage la Prairie area), his parents James & Jane, and the Setter families (Andrew/George, who settled at High Bluff/Poplar Point = Portage la Prairie region) were Red River Métis living in exactly the time (1820s–1870s) and place (Portage la Prairie + St. François Xavier / White Horse Plain) of the great organized buffalo hunts. In that world, subsistence participation in the buffalo economy was the norm for Métis of this class. But **no document ties a specific ancestor to the hunt** — treat any such claim as inference, not fact.

## PART 2 — THE BUFFALO HUNT (Portage la Prairie & St. François Xavier, 1820s–1870s)

### 2A. How the organized hunt worked **[VERIFIED — Wikipedia; Metis Heritage Project; Alexander Ross via Manitoba Pageant]**
- Two seasons: **summer (dried-meat) hunt** June–late July, and autumn hunt. Began in earnest by the 1820s; "by 1820 a permanent feature of life for all individuals on or near the Red River"; largest-scale hunts from c.1840; effectively ended 1877–78.
- **Three major Red River parties:** Pembina Métis, the St. Boniface / "Main River" party, and **the St. François Xavier Métis** (White Horse Plain) [3][4].
- Highly organized: **elected council**, "governor of the hunt," ten **captains**, each with ten soldiers, plus ten guides; the camp flag signals marching/camping; **captains** enforced strict rules (no running buffalo before the general order; offences punished by cutting saddles/clothes or flogging).
- All family members participated — men hunted, **women cut, preserved and processed meat**, made **pemmican** and dried meat (viande sèche), tallow and marrow (for the HBC pemmican trade).
- Scale: 1840 hunt = 1,210 Red River carts, ~620 hunters, ~650 women, 1,630 souls rendezvousing at Pembina; the St. François Xavier (White Horse Plain) group in 1849 alone = 700 Métis, 603 carts [4][14].
- **Hivernant (wintering) life:** families wintered on the plains/outposts, following the herds; by the late 1870s herds collapsed and the great hunts ended (Buffalo Lake, largest hivernant settlement, abandoned).
- **Battle of Grand Coteau (1851):** St. François Xavier hunting party (~200 carts, 67 hunters) led by **Jean-Baptiste Falcon**, repelled a much larger Sioux force — a defining event of the St. François Xavier buffalo-hunt community [4][19].

### 2B. DOCUMENTED FAMILY CONNECTION — William Peter Hallett (in-law) **[VERIFIED — Barkwell bio, metismuseum.ca/media/db/11103]**
**William Peter Hallett (1811–1873): Chief Scout and Leader of the "49th Rangers"** (compiled by Lawrence Barkwell, Louis Riel Institute):
- b. c1811 at Fort Vermilion, son of **Henry Hallett and Catherine Tenanse** (Henry's 8th child; Henry's 2nd of four mixed-blood wives). Baptized 18 Oct 1824 at Red River (Point Douglas).
- **"his prowess as a buffalo hunter was legend";** he was one of the **ten elected "Captains of the Hunt"** (Alexander Ross, *Red River Settlement*, p.246 documents his enforcing the hunt's camp rules); "During the 1860s, William was still the **elected leader of the English-speaking Half-Breed buffalo hunt** out of the Red River."
- Opponent of Riel in 1869–70 (arrested & jailed at Fort Garry); Chief Scout & leader of the **"49th Rangers"** (Métis guards/scouts of the International Boundary Commission, 1872–73).
- d. 27 Dec 1873 (self-inflicted gunshot, after years of erysipelas from 1869–70 imprisonment).
- **Relationship to THIS tree:** William Peter was the **half-brother of Henry Hallett Jr (1799–1871)**, who was the **father of Catherine Hallett**, wife of David Spence. So **William Peter Hallett was Catherine (Hallett) Spence's paternal half-uncle.** This is a **documented family (in-law) connection to the leadership of the buffalo hunt.** **[VERIFIED]**
- Source of Barkwell's biography credits **Heather Hallett, *Children of the Rivers* (1999: 221–250)** — a published family history of the Hallett line (another lead).

### 2C. Place-based likelihood (INFERENCE, but strongly supported)
- The **James Spence** household's world centred on the White Horse Plain (Jane's first husband Oman Norquay died there 1820) and St James/St John's — within the orbit of the **St. François Xavier buffalo-hunt community** [12].
- **David Spence** (1824–1885) lived at **Poplar Point** and represented St Anne's (Poplar Point); the Portage la Prairie / Poplar Point / High Bluff area was a major Métis buffalo-hunting & hivernant region in his lifetime (1830s–1870s) [2].
- **Andrew & George Setter** retired to and farmed in the **Poplar Point / High Bluff (Marquette)** area (Andrew d. 1870 Poplar Point; George's land grants 1879–83), squarely in the buffalo-hunt region [11][18].
- **Conclusion (INFERENCE):** It is **highly probable** that male members of the Spence and Setter blood lines participated in the subsistence/commercial buffalo economy of the era, given their Métis identity, residence, and dates. **But no document names any of them as a hunter.** Never assert a specific ancestor hunted without a source.
- **Caveat:** The "Spence, Nancy Mary (b.1797)" who appears in the Grand Coteau (1851) battle-family list [19] is the wife of James Whitford Sr and is a daughter of **Magnus Spence** — a **different** Red River Spence line from our **James Spence** line. Do not conflate.

## PART 3 — FURTHER RESEARCH AVENUES (ranked by likely value)

1. **Métis Scrip records (LAC), esp. for David & Catherine Spence (1875) and George Setter (1875).** The backbone record: applications state the applicant's father's name, birthplace, family, and often livelihood (e.g., "farmer, hunter, freighter"). Free at recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca; may hit a bot wall — use a normal browser if so. **Highest value** for closing branches AND for any documented "hunter"/buffalo activity.
2. **Métis Nation of Manitoba (MMF) / Louis Riel Institute genealogy & Citizenship Registry.** Don Norquay's family-verified-Métis claim and the "verified through the Métis Federation" remark in the forum suggest registry records exist. MMF Registry check needs the descendant's consent. LRI = Barkwell's research home.
3. **Oral history from living relatives (e.g., Mavis, Tracy).** The fastest way to close the gap on whether a family story about buffalo hunting exists, and to recover photos (Paula Montgomery holds a photo of David & Catherine). Family memory reliably beats the archives for the last ~3 generations.
4. **Manitoba vital statistics index** (vitalstats.gov.mb.ca, births ≤1925/marriages ≤1945/deaths ≤1955) — pin the recent Spence/Setter/Riggs generations and connect to the Flin Flon/Hamilton/Lau line.
5. **Gail Morin, *Company Men Vol. 18 – James Spence* (ISBN 978-1725559240) and Vol. 14 – Henry Hallett (ISBN 978-1720373421).** The Hallett volume likely documents William Peter Hallett's buffalo-hunt career and the in-law network; the Spence volume covers David Spence's descendants across 5 generations. Purchase/interlibrary loan (no free text online).
6. **Archives of Manitoba Keystone** — HBC personnel dossiers (Setter & Spence fur-trade service), parish records, photos (e.g., Portage la Prairie / St. François Xavier), journals. Search "Spence," "Setter," "Hallett," "Portage la Prairie," "White Horse Plain."
7. **DNA matches** — especially any matches into Riggs, Hallett, Norquay, or Batline/Whitford families; corroborates the endogamous Métis structure and can surface descendant cousins holding records.
8. **SHSB (Société historique de Saint-Boniface)** — parish registers + paid research service; good for pre-1882 sacramental records (the gap the vital-stats index can't cover).
9. **Gerhard Ens, *Homeland to Hinterland* (1996)** — the scholarly home of the White Horse Plain / Portage la Prairie buffalo-economy world; may name hunting families from these parishes (check index for Spence/Hallett/Setter).
10. **Census (1870, 1881, 1891, 1901, 1911)** — free on FamilySearch/Ancestry; confirms who was where during the hunt era and links generations.
11. **Manitoba Historical Society (mhs.mb.ca)** — local-history articles on Portage la Prairie / St. François Xavier and Métis families; search for these surnames.
