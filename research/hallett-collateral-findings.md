## 1. WILLIAM PETER HALLETT (1811–1873) — buffalo-hunt captain, Chief Scout 49th Rangers

**Identity & birth.** Born **about 1811 at Fort Vermilion, Alberta**, son of **Henry Hallett Sr (1773–1844)** and **Catherine Tenanse/Dansee** (Cree), the *second* of Henry Sr's four mixed-blood wives; William was Henry Sr's **eighth child**. **VERIFIED** (Metis Museum/Barkwell bio 11103; WikiTree Hallet-214; FamilySearch L8RF-WSQ; Red River Ancestry). Note: FamilySearch gives birth "about 1810"; WikiTree/RRA/Metis Museum say about 1811. **INFERENCE** on exact year — flag.

**Baptism.** Baptized 18 Oct 1824 at St John's Anglican Church, Red River Settlement by Rev. David Jones, after the family settled at Point Douglas. **VERIFIED** (Metis Museum bio; WikiTree).

**Career / historical significance:**
- Buffalo-hunt captain and leader of the **"49th Rangers"**, the Métis guards/scouts of the **International Boundary Commission (1872–1873)**. **VERIFIED** (Metis Museum bio; militarydatabase; WikiTree).
- **Opponent of Riel** during the **1869–70 Red River Resistance**. **VERIFIED** (Metis Museum bio).
- In **1872**, Boundary Commissioner Donald R. Cameron chose him as **Chief Scout** to recruit guards to protect the British contingent (from Sioux). Scouts provided own horses, received Spencer carbines; budget £4000/yr for 30 men. **VERIFIED** (Metis Museum bio).
- Had earlier guided **Capt. Donald Campbell** (Royal Artillery) escorting Wm. McDougall to Manitoba in **1869**. **VERIFIED** (Metis Museum bio).
- Sub-leaders under him: Adam McDonald (deputy), Francois Gosselin, Guillaume Lafournaise, William Gaddy; his nephew **William Pruden** served in the Rangers. **VERIFIED** (Metis Museum bio).
- Died winter 1873; D'Arcy East took over command. **VERIFIED** (Metis Museum bio).

**Death & burial.** Died **27 Dec 1873**, St James / St James, Winnipeg, MB. **VERIFIED** (FamilySearch L8RF-WSQ; WikiTree). Burial: WikiTree says **Portage La Prairie**; FamilySearch says **St James, MB**. **CONFLICT — needs resolution** (likely buried St James / St James Anglican, or note says Portage). **INFERENCE** pending cemetery check.

## 3. CORROBORATING / CONTEXT ON THE HALLETT FAMILY

### Henry Hallett Sr (1773–1844) and his children (Red River Ancestry, VERIFIED)
English-born HBC clerk (recruited by uncle Philip Turnor, 1793); worked Swan River, Buckingham/Island House, Fort Vermilion; joined NWC 1811; retired to Red River 1822 (Lots 249–250, Fort Rouge); died c1844. His children:
