## 🔥 PRIORITY #1 — The "Al Hamilton" NHL lead (STRONG match, connection NOT yet formally proven)

**WHO:** **Allan "Al" Guy Hamilton** (b. 20 Aug 1946, Flin Flon, MB — d. —) — former pro hockey defenceman.
**THE STORY (VERIFIED — a genuine hockey legend):**
- Signed as a teenager by the **New York Rangers**; played junior for the **Edmonton Oil Kings**, one of the rare players to play in the **Memorial Cup three straight years**, winning it in 1966 with a remarkable **82 points in 55 games** — drawing comparisons to **Bobby Orr**. ✅
- Played for the **NY Rangers** and **Buffalo Sabres** (NHL), then jumped to the WHA, where he became **captain of the Alberta/Edmonton Oilers** at age 26 for four seasons. ✅
- Selected for **Team Canada in the 1974 Summit Series against the Soviet Union.** ✅
- Still holds the **Edmonton Oiler WHA franchise records for most games (455) and most points (311) by a WHA Oiler**, and was franchise leader in games, assists, and points. ✅
- Part of a remarkable Flin Flon hockey generation — fellow Flin Flon-born NHLers include **Bobby Clarke, Eric Nesterenko, Gerry Hart, Reggie Leach.** (quantHockey ranks Al Hamilton #5 all-time NHLer born in Flin Flon.)
- His son **Steve Hamilton** also played hockey; another son **Andrew Hamilton** plays semi-pro in Australia. ✅
- Honoured by the **Manitoba Hockey Hall of Fame.**

**THE FAMILY CONNECTION — 🔥 LEAD (highly plausible, MUST confirm):**
- The family roster already lists an **"Alan Guy Hamilton"** as a living collateral — a child of **Lawrence Donald Hamilton (1912–1984) and Doris Alberta Setter**, and a sibling of **Mavis Irene Hamilton (b. 1933)**. ✅ (name + sibling set in roster)
- The NHL player is **"Allan Guy Hamilton," born 1946 in Flin Flon**, and the family's Hamilton generation settled in **Flin Flon** (Lawrence Donald died in Flin Flon 5 Feb 1984). The name match (Alan/Allan Guy) + birthplace (Flin Flon) + the family's actual presence in Flin Flon make it **highly likely the roster's "Alan Guy Hamilton" IS the NHL Al Hamilton — i.e., Bayard's maternal great-uncle** (younger brother of grandmother Mavis).

## ⭐ VERIFIED NOTABLE FIGURES (all direct ancestors/collaterals)

### David Spence (1824–1885) — first MLA for Poplar Point, Convention of Forty delegate ✅
- **WHO:** Bayard's 4th-great-grandfather (via Mary Ann Spence → Ella Riggs → Alan Setter → Doris Setter).
- Born 5 Sep 1824 at Red River, son of **James Spence** (HBC tailor) and **Jane Morwick**.
- **Delegate to the Convention of Forty (25 Jan–10 Feb 1870)** — the assembly that, alongside Riel, framed Manitoba's entry into Confederation; represented the parish of St. Anne's (Poplar Point). ✅
- **First MLA elected for Poplar Point**, 27 Dec 1870; served 1870–1874. Also **Justice of the Peace** for Poplar Point and Marquette. ✅
- **COLORFUL / TRAGIC STORY:** Died **16 Sep 1885 in Winnipeg hospital of "weakness arising from amputation of leg"** after being **accidentally shot in his own home by a neighbour who thought his gun was unloaded.** ⚑ A second, more sinister oral account (from a "Culture Canada" TV spot) claims he was shot in the dark during a heated dispute in his capacity as Justice of the Peace — lights went out, a shot fired, Spence hit. **The "accidental shooting by a neighbour" version is the standard one (Wikipedia + redriverancestry); the JP-dispute version is an unconfirmed but striking oral tradition worth noting.**
- Married **Catherine Hallett** (15 Feb 1844), daughter of **Henry Hallett** and **Catherine Parenteau** — linking the Spence and Hallett lines.
- **Sources:** Wikipedia "David Spence (Manitoba politician)"; MHS Memorable Manitobans spence_d; Metis Museum Convention of Forty (Barkwell); redriverancestry.ca SPENCE-DAVID-1834.

### John Norquay (1841–1889) — Manitoba's 5th Premier & FIRST INDIGENOUS PREMIER of a Canadian province ✅
- **WHO:** The tree's context already flags him as "Manitoba's first Métis Premier." He is connected to this family **through Jane Morwick, the mother of David Spence — Jane Morwick was John Norquay's grandmother** (Norquay's mother was Isabella Truthwaite, daughter of Jane Morwick). David Spence and John Norquay were therefore **half-uncles/nephew by marriage (both descend from the Spence/Morwick house).** ✅
- Premier 1878–1887 (5th premier); held Premier + President of Executive Council + Provincial Treasurer + Railway Commissioner. Born at St. Andrews; raised by his grandmother (the widow Spence) after his parents died young; won a scholarship at St. John's Collegiate School; farmed at **High Bluff** (a family place-name!). ✅
- **"The Honourable John Norquay: Indigenous Premier, Canadian Statesman"** (2022 biography) recounts his 10 trips to Ottawa fighting for a better deal for Manitoba in federation. ✅
- Made **10 trips to the nation's capital** during his premiership. ✅
- **Sources:** Wikipedia "John Norquay"; MHS Memorable Manitobans norquay_j; cbha.ca; canadashistory.ca; Winnipeg Free Press 18 Aug 2023.

### William Peter Hallett (1811–1873) — buffalo-hunt captain, chief scout of the "49th Rangers" ✅
- **WHO:** Almost certainly **the brother of Catherine Hallett (David Spence's wife)** — both children of **Henry Hallett**. (See LEAD below — this sibling link is strong but needs formal confirmation.) That would make William Peter Hallett Bayard's **direct great-great-great-uncle (4th-great uncle).**
- **THE STORY (VERIFIED, colorful):**
  - Born at **Fort Vermilion** in 1811, son of Henry Hallett; moved to Red River 1824. ✅
  - For years the elected leader of the **English "Plains Hunt" / Red River buffalo hunt** — "his prowess as a buffalo hunter was legend." ✅
  - **Chief Scout and leader of the "49th Rangers,"** a group of ~30 Métis armed scouts. ✅
  - **Helped free James Stewart from jail in 1863.** ✅
  - Employed 1869 as **guide/interpreter for one of Colonel Dennis's survey parties**, helping Dennis evade Métis patrols. ✅
  - **Taken prisoner by Louis Riel**; engaged in a famous **word-slinging match with Riel** the night John Schultz and others escaped Fort Garry; ended up **in irons**, bailed out for **£450** on 12 Feb 1870, later compensated for property and imprisonment. ✅
  - **Tragic end:** According to testimony at the 1874 Ambroise Lépine trial, his **health was broken by confinement and he committed suicide 27 Dec 1873.** ✅
- **SOURCES:** MHS Memorable Manitobans hallett_w; Metis Museum "William Peter Hallett (1811–1873): Chief Scout and Leader of the 49th Rangers"; facebook Metis History Tidbit; and a **new 2025 biography** — Audrhea Lande, *On the Hunt for William Hallett: Discovering a Forgotten Métis Leader* (Your Nickel's Worth Publishing, 2025).
- **🔥 LEAD to confirm:** The Hallett sibling link. Catherine Hallett's father is given as **Henry Hallett (1799–1871)** by redriverancestry; William Peter's father is also **Henry Hallett** (MHS). Redriverancestry also documents a **Henry Hallett (1773–1844)** who married a **Catherine Parenteau** and whose son Henry also married a Catherine Parenteau — so there are (at least) two Henrys and two Catherine Parenteaus to untangle. **Confirm whether Catherine (Spence) and William Peter were siblings — if yes, the famous buffalo-hunt captain is a direct great-uncle.**

### William Kennedy (1814–1890) — Métis Arctic explorer who searched for Franklin ✅
- **WHO:** Brother of **Isabella Kennedy**, who was the mother of **Elizabeth Setter** — the wife of **Premier John Norquay.** (Kennedy was therefore Elizabeth's uncle.)
- **THE STORY (VERIFIED):** A prominent **Métis Arctic explorer**; commanded **Lady Franklin's 1851–52 expedition in search of Sir John Franklin's lost expedition** aboard the ketch *Prince Albert*. Though he did not find Franklin, he mapped part of the Arctic, brought his crew home safely, and promoted Indigenous rights/education. ❄️
- **FAMILY CONNECTION — 🔥 LEAD:** Elizabeth Setter (Norquay's wife, b.1842) was daughter of **George Setter and Isabella Kennedy.** The family's own Setter line runs through **Roderick McKenzie Setter (b.1856), son of George Setter and Jessie Ellen Campbell.** **If (or how) these two "George Setter" figures are related — possibly one man with two wives, or two cousins — is a reconciliation worth doing.** If the two George Setters are the same or closely related, then **William Kennedy the Franklin explorer is connected to Bayard's Setter line** — a spectacular family story. ⚠ **Flag for reconciliation.**
- **Sources:** Wikipedia "William Kennedy (explorer)"; DCB biographi.ca kennedy_william_11E; The Canadian Encyclopedia "William Kennedy"; arcticfocus.org.
