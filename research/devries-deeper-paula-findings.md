## 2. deVries surname origin & Netherlands question

### Surname meaning — ✅ (dictionary + genealogy)
- **"de Vries"** = Dutch/Frisian **ethnic surname meaning "the Frisian,"** formed with the definite article *de* + *Vries* (from Friesland). One of the **most common surnames in the Netherlands**; many inhabitants of Friesland assumed it under Napoleon (1811). — ✅ FamilySearch name-meaning (Dictionary of American Family Names, Hanks) on the Gerhard page; ✅ https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Vries
- FamilySearch also lists related forms: **De Fries, Defrees, Defreese** — same "the Frisian" root.

### But: this branch came from EAST FRISIA, GERMANY, not the Netherlands ✅/⚑
- **East Frisia (Ostfriesland)** is the **historically Frisian, Low-German corner of northwest Germany bordering the Netherlands** — NOT the Netherlands proper. The villages **Dyksterhusen, Ditzumerhammrich, Bunderhammrich, Weener** are in the Jemgum/Leer district of Lower Saxony (east of the Ems, across from the Dutch province of Groningen).
- Gerhard (b. Dyksterhusen 1901), his mother Trienje Pommer (b. Ditzumerhammrich 1863), and Geeske Oltrop (b. Ditzumerverlaat 1910, parents in Bunderhammrich/Weener) **all trace to this same small corner of East Frisia.**
- **Conclusion:** the **surname** is Dutch/Frisian, but the **family's place of origin** is the German province of East Frisia. Answering "do the de Vries have roots in the Netherlands?" — they have **Frisian heritage** via German East Frisia, **not** a direct Dutch residence; no record ties this branch to the Netherlands proper. (The name is common all across the historic Friesland region which straddles the modern Netherlands/Germany border.)

### When did they come to Canada? — INFERENCE (well-supported)
- **By 1929 at the latest:** Gerhard married Geeske Oltrop in Ochre River RM, MB (1929). Their first child Trienette was b. 1929, John b. 1930. The "Canada Census, 1931" is cited on both Gerhard's and John's FamilySearch pages → **family in Canada by 1931.**
- **Father Leewe died in Germany in 1926** (4 May 1926) and is **not** buried at Turtle River → he never came to Canada.
- **Mother Trienje came to Canada** and died in **Ste. Rose du Lac, MB in 1937** (near Ochre River), buried at Turtle River.
- **INFERENCE:** the de Vries family (adult children, incl. Gerhard ~25) emigrated from East Frisia to the Ochre River area of Manitoba in the **~1926–1929 window**, likely in the wake of the father's 1926 death and matching the documented post-WWI East Frisian emigration wave to the Dauphin/Ochre River area. Exact passenger/immigration records (paywalled/LAC digitized) were **not** opened.

### The two East Frisian families intermarried in Canada
- The **de Vries** and **Oltrop** families came from the **same East Frisian villages** and both settled around Ochre River, MB; Gerhard de Vries married Geeske Oltrop there in 1929. They were almost certainly neighbors/kin in Ostfriesland who emigrated together in the 1920s. (⚠ secondary; consistent with all records.)
