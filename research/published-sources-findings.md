## 1. GAIL MORIN — "Company Men" series (Red River / fur-trade genealogies)

### 1a. Company Men — Volume 18: James Spence  [VERIFIED — the key volume]
This is the volume the family tree project knows as "Company Men: James Spence." It is **volume 18** of Gail Morin's *Company Men* series.

- **Author:** Gail Morin
- **Title:** *Company Men – Volume 18 – James Spence*
- **Publisher / imprint:** Self-published via Amazon KDP (CreateSpace/Amazon print-on-demand), 2017–2018
- **ISBN-10:** 1725559242 · **ISBN-13:** 978-1725559240
- **Formats:** Paperback and Kindle eBook
- **Obtain:**
  - Amazon.com → https://www.amazon.com/Company-Men-18-James-Spence/dp/1725559242
  - Amazon.ca / Amazon.co.uk / Amazon.ie (same ISBN)
  - Resellers: ThriftBooks, BooksRun, eBay (search "Company Men 18 James Spence Morin")
- **Confirmed content:** A Facebook announcement by Gail Morin in the *Métis Family Resources* group states she "just finished the 18th Company Men book – James Spence and his descendants," available in paperback and Kindle. The *Company Men* series devotes one volume to a male HBC/fur-trade ancestor and his known descendants across ~five generations. James Spence (1778–1857), the HBC tailor, is the subject; the volume covers his line including David Spence (1824–1885), Catherine Hallett, and connections to the Norquay (Jane Morwick) line.
- **Page count:** The task description says "~200+ pages." The comparable Hallett volume (Vol. 14) runs 408 pages. The exact page count of Vol. 18 was **not independently verified** — assume 250–400 pages.
- **Online full text:** **None located.** The book is **not** on Archive.org full-text (searched archive.org; no "Company Men James Spence" hit). No legal free PDF/HTML text exists online. Purchase (Amazon paperback/Kindle) or interlibrary loan are the routes.

## 2. GAIL MORIN — "Métis Families: A Genealogical Compendium"

The broad, family-indexed companion series (alphabetical by surname) — useful for **Batt, Setter, Spence, Hallett, Riggs**.

- **Original 2-volume edition:**
  - Morin, Gail. *Métis Families: A Genealogical Compendium*. Pawtucket, RI: Quintin Publications, 1995/1996. (2 vols.)
  - Library holdings: Minnesota Historical Society (libguides.mnhs.org/metis/books), UCalgary Taylor Family Digital Library, Selkirk College, etc. Search WorldCat for "Métis families genealogical compendium Morin."
- **11-volume expanded series (Amazon):**
  - Self-published via CreateSpace/Amazon, c. 2015–2017. Volumes cover surname ranges; **Volume 10 (Racette–Stevenson)** is confirmed to include **Andrew Setter** (catalogue description of Vol. 10 lists "…Andrew Setter, Edward Sharpe, James Short, George Simpson…"). Volume 2 covers the B–C ranges (Batt likely falls in an earlier volume; the original Vol. 1 covered A–C).
  - Obtain: Amazon.com / Amazon.ca (e.g. *Metis Families Volume 10 Racette–Stevenson*, ISBN 978-1530743742); ThriftBooks; eBay.
- **Summary:** A surname-arranged compendium of Métis families from the fur trade and Red River Settlement. **[INFERENCE]** Contains entries for Spence, Setter, Batt, Hallett, and Riggs families, with marriage/descendant data sourced from parish registers and HBC records. Verify which volume covers each surname before relying on it.

## 3. DICTIONARY OF CANADIAN BIOGRAPHY (biographi.ca) — DCB entries

### 3a. Isaac Batt  [VERIFIED — full quote]
- **Author:** Jennifer S. H. Brown
- **Title:** "BATT, ISAAC," in *Dictionary of Canadian Biography*, vol. 4, University of Toronto/Université Laval, 2003–; accessed 2026-08-12.
- **URL:** https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/batt_isaac_4E.html
- **Key biographical details:**
  - "Isaac Batt first contracted with the Hudson's Bay Company in 1754 to serve as a labourer at York Factory (Man.) for five years at £10 per annum."
  - By 1768, "Batt, who travelled inland yearly, had established himself as one of the company's more influential servants among the Indians in the vicinity of present day The Pas, Man., and was considered 'a very honest good Servant.'"
  - "While Batt remained a labourer, canoeman, and steersman at wages never surpassing £20 a year…" — he was illiterate and could not keep journals.
  - "His usefulness inland as canoeman, trader, and hunter continued, however, well into the 1780s…" (1791: "An old Servant – almost worn out.")
  - Death: "the unfortunate end of Isaac Batt… He was the first HBC servant to be killed by Indians in the Saskatchewan area." (c. 1791)
  - Family: "Batt was known to have had a native family as early as 1777, and at his death he left descendants in the fur trade country." (i.e., the Margaret "Nestichio" Batt line)
- **Buffalo-hunt / livelihood mention:** The DCB entry does **NOT** explicitly mention the buffalo hunt. His livelihood is described as **labourer, canoeman, steersman, inland trader, and hunter** (HBC servant). Any buffalo-hunt connection for the Batt family is an inference from the Métis context, not stated in DCB.

### 3b. John Norquay  [VERIFIED — full quote; directly ties to the Spence family]
- **Author:** Gerald Friesen
- **Title:** "NORQUAY, JOHN," in *Dictionary of Canadian Biography*, vol. 11, University of Toronto/Université Laval, 2003–; accessed 2026-08-12.
- **URL:** https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/norquay_john_11E.html
- **Key biographical details (verbatim):**
  - "NORQUAY, JOHN, politician; b. 8 May 1841 near St Andrews (Man.), the fifth of six children of John Norquay and Isabella Truthwaite; m. in June 1862 Elizabeth Setter at Parkdale (Man.), and they had three daughters and five sons; d. 5 July 1889 in Winnipeg, Man." — **Note the marriage to Elizabeth Setter** (the Setter line).
  - **Spence connection:** "His mother died in 1843 when he was two and his father in 1849. He was then raised by his paternal grandmother, **Mrs James Spence**, and by his teachers at the Anglican St John's Collegiate School in Red River." — i.e., **Jane Morwick, wife of James Spence Jr (1778–1857)**, is confirmed as Norquay's grandmother.
  - Livelihood: "In the mid 1860s they went to High Bluff near **Portage la Prairie** where Norquay engaged in farming. During lean times he traded furs on Lake Manitoba; he never achieved prosperity. Elizabeth… recalled that during these years they lived close to the land, in a log house with a mud fireplace and chimney…"
  - Career: taught in Red River; elected MLA for High Bluff 1870; **Premier of Manitoba 1878–1887**; ousted Dec. 1887; d. 5 July 1889.
- **Buffalo-hunt / livelihood mention:** DCB does **not** use the phrase "buffalo hunt." His livelihood is given as **farmer and fur trader**. (Norquay's era post-dates the great buffalo hunts; the buffalo-hunt economy of the White Horse Plain/Portage Métis is best covered by Gerhard Ens, §4.)

### 3c. David Spence (1824–1885)  [VERIFIED — NO DCB ENTRY EXISTS]
- **There is NO Dictionary of Canadian Biography entry for David Spence.** Searches of biographi.ca return only THOMAS SPENCE (1832–1900), who is a **different, unrelated** individual (moved to Portage la Prairie 1867 but is not of this James Spence line). Do **not** cite the Thomas Spence DCB entry for David.
- **Best scholarly/biographical sources for David Spence:**
  1. **Lawrence J. Barkwell, "Spence, David" (1824–1885),** biography PDF, Gabriel Dumont Institute / Virtual Museum of Métis History and Culture, copyright 2014-12-03. URL: https://www.metismuseum.ca/resource.php/15059 (PDF: metismuseum.ca/media/document.php/15059.David%20Spence.pdf). [VERIFIED to exist]
  2. **Red River Ancestry** detailed family page: https://www.redriverancestry.ca/SPENCE-DAVID-1834.php [VERIFIED — see quote below]
  3. **Wikipedia, "David Spence (Manitoba politician)"**: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Spence_(Manitoba_politician) [VERIFIED]
  4. **Gabriel Dumont Institute, "Manitoba's Provisional Government of 1870: The Convention of Forty"** (Convention of Forty delegate list, 1870): metismuseum.ca/resource.php/12539 [VERIFIED]
- **Key facts on David Spence (from Red River Ancestry, corroborated by Wikipedia):**
  - b. 5 Sep 1824, St John's Parish (Red River); son of **James Spence (1778–1857)** and **Jane Morwick**; his mother Jane was grandmother to Premier **John Norquay**.
  - m. 15 Feb 1844 Catherine Hallett (1824–1880), daughter of **Henry Hallett and Catherine Parenteau**, at St John's; witnesses James Spence and John Sutherland.
  - **Convention of Forty (Jan–Feb 1870):** Métis delegate representing the parish of **St Anne's (Poplar Point)**.
  - **First MLA for Poplar Point**, elected 27 Dec 1870, served **1870–1874** in the Legislative Assembly of Manitoba.
  - Justice of the Peace for Poplar Point and Marquette; lived on River Lot 62, Poplar Point.
  - 1875: claimed Half-breed Scrip (David and Catherine).
  - d. 16 Sep 1885 in Winnipeg hospital of "weakness arising from amputation of leg" after being accidentally shot in his home by a neighbour (per Barkwell/Red River Ancestry; a second, less-likely version ties the shooting to JP duties).
  - Children (7): John/Jake, Ellen, Charles David, Jane, **Mary Ann (Marion) (m. Ernest Charles Riggs 1887)**, Harriet, William.
- **Buffalo-hunt / livelihood mention for David Spence:** None of the located biographies mention David Spence as a buffalo hunter. His documented livelihood/roles are **farmer, MLA, and justice of the peace**. Any buffalo-hunt role for him is **[INFERENCE/unverified]**.

## 4. OTHER BOOKS / THESES / ARTICLES (Red River Métis of Portage la Prairie & St. Francois Xavier)

### 4a. Gerhard J. Ens — "Homeland to Hinterland"  [VERIFIED — the key scholarly monograph for the buffalo-hunt economy]
- Ens, Gerhard John. *Homeland to Hinterland: The Changing Worlds of the Red River Métis in the Nineteenth Century.* Toronto: University of Toronto Press, 1996. ISBN 0-8020-7822-2 (paper).
- **Obtain:** Amazon; Google Books (books.google.com id A02WjWrFxQoC); UToronto Press; full text circulating on dokumen.pub; university libraries (UCalgary, UManitoba, MNHS).
- **Summary:** The definitive social/economic history of the Red River Métis, focusing on the **White Horse Plain (St. François Xavier) and Portage la Prairie** Métis who lived by the **buffalo hunt** in the 1830s–1860s and transitioned to farming/settled life. **[INFERENCE]** Contains documented mentions of Spence/Hallett/Setter families among the White Horse Plain buffalo-hunting community. **This is the primary scholarly home for the buffalo-hunt livelihood** of this family's world.

### 4b. R. E. Swan — "Pembina and the Origins of the Red River Valley Métis"  [VERIFIED]
- Swan, R. E. *Pembina and the Origins of the Red River Valley Métis.* PhD dissertation, 2003. Open access via University of Manitoba MSpace: https://mspace.lib.umanitoba.ca/bitstreams/88e9672a-bf26-4c46-882d-f6efe05428f7/download
- **Summary:** Origins and migrations of Red River Valley Métis (includes buffalo-hunt economy and settlement history relevant to Poplar Point/Portage context).

### 4c. Manitoba Historical Society — Transactions & Manitoba History  [VERIFIED resource]
- MHS Transactions archive (searchable online): https://www.mhs.mb.ca/docs/transactions/index.shtml
- *Manitoba History* (1980–2019) and *Prairie History* (2020–): https://www.mhs.ca/research/manitoba-history/
- Notable related article: "Some Comments on the Social Origins of the Riel Protest" (MHS Transactions, Series 3): https://www.mhs.mb.ca/docs/transactions/3/socialorigins.shtml
- **[INFERENCE]** MHS has published local-history articles on Portage la Prairie and Red River Métis families; search the MHS index for "Spence," "Portage la Prairie," "White Horse Plain" for specific pieces.

### 4d. Lawrence J. Barkwell — "Metis Dictionary of Biography"  [VERIFIED — key genealogical reference]

## 5. HONEST GAPS & CAVEATS

1. **"Company Men: James Spence" is Volume 18** of the series (ISBN 978-1725559240), available from Amazon (paperback + Kindle). **No online/free full text exists**; it is not on Archive.org.
2. **No DCB entry for David Spence or James Spence.** The DCB only covers Isaac Batt (Vol. 4) and John Norquay (Vol. 11) among the requested names. David Spence's biography is best sourced from **Barkwell/Metis Museum**, **Red River Ancestry**, and **Wikipedia**.
3. **Buffalo-hunt mentions in DCB are absent.** Isaac Batt's DCB livelihood = labourer/canoeman/steersman/trader/**hunter**; John Norquay's = **farmer/fur-trader**. Neither mentions the buffalo hunt by name. The buffalo-hunt world of this family is documented in **Gerhard Ens, *Homeland to Hinterland*** (White Horse Plain/Portage la Prairie Métis).
4. **No dedicated Setter/Riggs/Batt "Company Men" volume** was located; these families are likely covered inside the Spence/Hallett volumes and in *Métis Families*.
5. Item page counts and "likely content" of Morin volumes are **[INFERENCE]** until checked against the physical books.
